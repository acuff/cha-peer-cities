Peer cities were identified with k-nearest neighbors (KNN) in z-score standardized variable space using 43 demographic, economic, social, housing, geographic, fiscal, and workforce variables. The 25 cities shown are Chattanooga’s nearest structural neighbors among municipalities (US Census places) with 2024 populations between 150,000–500,000 and sufficient data availability (263 cities, total). The approach extends the Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago’s Peer City Identification Tool (PCIT) with additional data sources and analytic refinements.

Data sources: American Community Survey 1-year estimates (2024 and 2014), 2000 Decennial Census, 1980 IPUMS National Historical Geographic Information System (NHGIS) STF3, Census TIGER/Line shapefiles, 2022 Census of Governments State and Local Government Finance, 2024 Annual Survey of Public Employment & Payroll (ASPEP), and respective cities’ annual comprehensive financial reports (ACFRs).

Notes
  • Structural distance is the Euclidean distance between a city and Chattanooga in standardized (z-score) variable space (i.e. the square root of the summed squared differences across all variables) where lower values indicate greater similarity.
  • Fiscal variables use city-submitted data and estimates and are sensitive to differences in governmental structure and services; places with non-standard structures (e.g. consolidated city-counties, independent cities, etc.) were excluded.
  • Chattanooga’s intergovernmental (IGR) revenue share excludes local-option sales taxes. For this analysis, LOSTs were considered own-source revenue, though classified as IGR in the ACFR.
  • Government finance values included in the City Data tab (revenue, expenditure, construction, and debt per capita) are shown in dollars, converted from the Census of Governments’ data; payroll figures are monthly (March reference period, per ASPEP data). Data included in the Financial Data tab were derived from the FY 2025 ACFRs, where available; though some municipalities operating on a fiscal year beginning in January did not have available audits for FY 2025. In this case, FY 2024 was used.

Listed below are the variables used to identify Chattanooga’s peer cities. Each entry gives the column name as it appears in the Peer Cities Data table and a brief description (where applicable). Variables are grouped as they appear in the comparison chart’s dropdown menu by PCIT theme.

Baseline
  • Total population — total population of the city.
  • City share of MSA population — the city’s population relative to that of its metropolitan statistical area (MSA). USed as the PCIT’s universal control variable, applied across all four themes.
  • Median household income — median household income in 2024 dollars.
  • Median monthly housing cost — median monthly cost of housing.

Equity (PCIT Theme)

  • Dissimilarity index (White/non-White) — degree of segregation between non-Hispanic White populations and all other racial and ethnic groups, ranging from 0 (total integration) to 1 (total segregation), measuring the distribution of minority and majority members across census tracts within an urban area (Massey and Denton, 1987).
  • Non-Hispanic White share — proportion identifying as White, non-Hispanic.
  • Gini index — wage-based Gini coefficient of income inequality.
  • Gini change, 10-yr — absolute change in the Gini coefficient, 2014–2024.
  • Poverty rate (persons) — percentage of the population living below the poverty rate threshold.
  • Poverty rate change, 10-yr — percentage-point change in the poverty rate, 2014–2024.
  • Associate’s degree or higher — residents age 25 and older holding an associate’s degree or higher.

Resilience (PCIT Theme)

  • Unemployment rate — unemployment rate for residents age 16 or older.
  • Labor force participation rate — labor force participation among the population age 16 or older.
  • LFPR change, 10-yr — percentage-point change in labor force participation, 2014–2024.
  • Manufacturing employment share — percentage of employed workers in manufacturing or related fields.
  • Manufacturing change, 1980–2024 — percentage-point change in the labor share of manufacturing.
  • Median HH income growth, 10-yr — percent change in median household income, 2014–2024.

Outlook (PCIT Theme)

  • Foreign-born share — perecntage of residents born outside the United States.
  • Households with children — percentage of households with members under age 18.
  • Population age 20–64 — percentage of residents between the ages of 20 and 64.
  • Population change, 2000–2024 — percentage difference in population between 2000 and 2024.

Housing (PCIT Theme)

  • Home value-to-income ratio — median value of owner-occupied homes relative to median household income.
  • Homeownership rate — percentage of occupied units that are owner-occupied.
  • Rent-burdened households — renters spending more than 30 percent of gross monthly income on rent.
  • Vacancy rate — percentage of residential units that are unoccupied.
  • Housing built before 1980 — portion of housing units constructed before 1980.
  • Median gross rent — median monthly gross rent.

Geography (Census TIGER/Line shapefiles)

  • Land area (sq mi) — city land area in square miles.
  • Population density (per sq mi) — residents per square mile of land area.

Government Finance (Census of Governments, State and Local Government Finance, 2022)

  • Revenue per capita — total revenue divided by population.
  • Expenditure per capita — total expenditure divided by population.
  • Construction spending per capita — capital construction outlays divided by population.
  • Debt outstanding per capita — total debt outstanding divided by population.
  • Debt-to-revenue ratio — debt outstanding relative to revenue.
  • Police share of expenditure — police spending as a percentage of total expenditures.
  • Fire share of expenditure — fire spending as a percentage of total expenditures.
  • Highways share of expenditure — highway spending as a percentage of total expenditures.
  • Parks share of expenditure — parks spending as a percentage of total expenditures.

Government Employment (Annual Survey of Public Employment & Payroll, 2024)

  • City employees per 1,000 residents — total municipal employees relative to population.
  • Monthly payroll per capita — total monthly payroll divided by population.
  • Avg. full-time monthly pay — average monthly pay for full-time employees.
  • Police share of employees — police as a percentage of the full-time workforce.
  • Fire share of employees — fire as a percentage of the full-time workforce.

The Financial Data tab draws on figures compiled from each city’s annual comprehensive financial report (ACFR), rather than the Census of Governments data used to identify Chattanooga’s peer cities.

Revenue
  • Revenue per capita — total revenue divided by population.
  • Intergovernmental revenue per capita — revenue received from federal, state, and other governments, divided by population.
  • Intergovernmental share of revenue — intergovernmental revenue as a percentage of total revenue.
Expenditure
  • Expenditures per capita — total expenditures divided by population.
  • Police spending per capita — police expenditures divided by population.
  • Fire spending per capita — fire expenditures divided by population.
  • Public works spending per capita — public works expenditures divided by population.
  • Parks spending per capita — parks and recreation expenditures divided by population.
Debt
  • Debt outstanding per capita — total outstanding debt divided by population.
Workforce
  • Full-time equivalents per 1,000 residents — municipal staffing level relative to population. (Note: Some cities’ employment numbers are headcount, not FTEs; those operating with a municipal school system excludes educators.)
